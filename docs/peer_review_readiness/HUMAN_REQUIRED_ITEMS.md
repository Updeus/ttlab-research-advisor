# Human-required items

This list is intentionally limited to the two audit findings that repository
evidence cannot resolve. Engineering effort, AI-assisted source review, and
conservative claim reduction are not treated as reasons to defer work to a
human. Neither item below prevents local technical demonstration. RIGHTS-001
prevents unqualified public exposure or distribution of affected records;
SUBMISSION-001 prevents formal manuscript submission.

## RIGHTS-001 — Rights and accountable public operation

Codex can enforce a fail-closed publication ledger, hide unapproved records,
exclude restricted files from releases, and preserve corrections. It cannot
create the underlying legal or institutional authority.

The project's TTLAB authorization and laboratory-corpus-use authorization are
accepted facts and are not reopened here. If a formal release process requires
documentary evidence of their exact scope, the human task is to supply the
existing record—not to seek or invent a new authorization.

Minimum external input for public operation or distribution:

1. Any existing dated authorization record and its scope, if required by the
   institution or release decision.
2. A per-paper decision covering public metadata, snippets or extracted text,
   source links, and PDF redistribution. Silence means the material remains not
   exposed and not released; it is not converted into a legal conclusion.
3. An accountable institutional contact and approved rules for retention,
   correction or appeal, access requests, incidents, and backups.
4. Editorial decisions for any author identity, title/PDF match, or derived
   topic that is intended to become public. Ambiguous identities must remain
   unresolved until that decision exists.

Why this cannot be inferred: authorization to conduct the TTLAB project and use
the laboratory corpus is not a university research-ethics approval, a public
deployment approval, or a blanket licence to redistribute third-party PDFs.
The repository therefore defaults every public-use decision to not exposed or
pending review.

Human acceptance evidence is the applicable authorization/rights record,
per-paper decisions, and named operational ownership. Separately, technical
acceptance evidence is an automated anonymous-route and sanitized-release scan
showing that only explicitly allowed material is exposed. The technical scan
cannot prove the underlying rights.

## SUBMISSION-001 — Institution- and venue-specific submission approval

Codex can compile and locally preflight both manuscripts, enforce page/reference
constraints, remove generic PDF-profile defects, and apply supplied metadata.
It cannot decide or attest institution- and venue-specific facts.

Minimum external input:

1. The approved thesis template or exact title-page/declaration language,
   official degree and TTLAB names, supervisor/co-supervisor names, submission
   date, and any required student identifier.
2. Author-approved title, author order, affiliations, acknowledgements,
   funding, and conflict-of-interest statement.
3. The target IEEE venue, review versus camera-ready mode, conference profile or
   code, copyright notice, and venue AI-use policy.
4. Confirmation that the repository's AI-assistance disclosure and authorship
   representation comply with the applicable institutional and venue rules.
5. The authorized venue checker or PDF eXpress result after local preflight.

Why this cannot be inferred: those values are decisions or attestations by the
author, supervisor, institution, rights holder, or venue. Inventing them would
misrepresent the submission.

Acceptance evidence: completed approved front matter and author block,
funding/COI and AI-use attestations, a selected venue profile, supervisor/author
sign-off, and the venue-generated PDF report.

## Optional future human study — not an open remediation issue

The present work makes no human-usefulness, supervisor-fit, accessibility-
usability, or practical-feasibility claim. A future study would first require an
institutional ethics determination and an approved recruitment, consent,
privacy, retention, training, and adjudication protocol. No approval or
participant result should be added unless a real record exists.
