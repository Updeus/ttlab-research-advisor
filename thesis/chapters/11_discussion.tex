\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

\section{Traceability is an engineering property, not an accuracy score}

The platform now preserves paper, chunk, page/section, provider, warning, timestamp, and review state through its principal workflows. Complete manifest coverage eliminates the earlier ambiguity between active index files and stale database flags. These are meaningful engineering results because they make an output auditable and a stale index detectable. They do not make the output correct.

The QA evaluation illustrates the distinction. Extractive composition largely avoided unsupported statements by copying supported passages, yet claim--citation choice and answer-point coverage were much weaker. It selected off-topic evidence and omitted requested information even when every sentence carried a locator. Traceability reduces the cost of diagnosis; it does not substitute for diagnosis.

\section{Simple lexical retrieval remained competitive}

Keyword retrieval produced the highest held-out MRR and nDCG@10 point estimates. Learned dense was close, but the study was not designed as an equivalence or non-inferiority test. The tuned hybrid ranked below both and did not yield a corrected significant gain even over feature hashing. This agrees with the broader lesson of heterogeneous retrieval benchmarks: learned representations are not automatically superior for every corpus \cite{thakur2021beir}.

Several features may explain the keyword result without establishing causality. The laboratory questions and paper titles share specialised vocabulary; relevant-paper labels may favour explicit lexical matches; the corpus is small; and the candidate pool is already rich. The post-selection ablation showing a stronger cumulative configuration is hypothesis-generating, not an invitation to retune on the held-out set. A future release needs a larger preregistered test partition and hard paraphrase cases.

\section{Abstention is the principal end-to-end safety gap}

All retrieval modes returned a positive result on the single held-out unanswerable case, and the answerer failed to abstain on all four QA unanswerable cases. This limitation is more consequential than small ranking differences: a user may receive a fluent, supported but irrelevant answer to a question the corpus cannot answer. An abstention mechanism should therefore combine calibrated top-score/margin signals, evidence coverage, corpus-scope classification, and explicit fallback wording, then be evaluated on a substantially larger unanswerable set.

\section{Recommendation structure is stronger than recommendation validation}

The extension schema successfully separates baseline evidence, explicit versus inferred gaps, system suggestions, MVP/stretch scope, risk, skills, data assumptions, and evaluation plans. All reviewed outputs retained those boundaries. However, every feasibility judgment was partial, and the relevance difference over an evidence-only baseline crossed zero. The full finder should be presented as a structured brainstorming aid, not as proof that an idea is novel, feasible, suitable for a degree, or approved by a supervisor. The evidence-only mode is valuable because it lets a cautious user stop before generation.

\section{Controlled discovery favors inspectability}

The topic comparison exposes a practical trade-off. Dense prototypes increased coverage and recall but reduced precision. The controlled vocabulary had higher precision, slightly higher F1, deterministic rules, and an editable governance surface. That profile better suits a public laboratory archive where over-attributing a topic to a researcher can cause reputational harm. The remaining 13 identity candidates show why automatic name merging is intentionally conservative. Publication-derived relations should remain qualified even after identity resolution.

\section{Review and governance as first-class data}

Append-only AI review events close the earlier empty-governance state and demonstrate deterministic idempotence. The design does not conflate that activity with human approval: reproducible outputs are AI-reviewed, while configuration-incomplete historical answers remain needs-reprocess. Authentication, actor roles, chain hashes, and protected status transitions make future human review attributable. A deployed institution still needs operational ownership, identity lifecycle, correction response times, and legal/privacy decisions.

\section{Interface conformance is not human validation}

The requirement--API--view--test trace shows that the implemented public and protected flows preserve evidence, warnings, request-lifecycle boundaries, authorization, and correction state under the tested conditions. It also exposes what those checks cannot measure. A semantic label can exist while remaining confusing; keyboard focus can be visible while a full screen-reader workflow remains inefficient; and a Finder warning can render while a student still over-trusts the suggestion. RQ7 therefore closes an engineering traceability gap without converting automated conformance into usability, accessibility, trust, or educational evidence.

\section{Reproducibility with rights constraints}

An exact full-corpus replication requires lawful access to the third-party papers; publishing their text would improve convenience by violating the evidential and rights boundary. The sanitized release therefore favours computational transparency over unrestricted data redistribution. It includes code, schemas, labels, sanitized results, prompts/configurations, hashes, code-native figure sources, and instructions, while requiring recipients to reacquire permitted documents. Interface screenshots are excluded because rendered source/answer text cannot be field-redacted reliably. The external sanity harness mapped open JATS/XML documents into the production chunker's extraction contract. It did not exercise the primary PDF-ingestion path, and a few life-science articles do not establish domain transfer.
