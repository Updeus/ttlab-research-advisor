\chapter{Research Questions and Objectives}
\label{ch:rqs}

The research questions separate data integrity, retrieval, answer quality, recommendation, discovery/review, reproduction, and interface governance. The earlier five-question structure overloaded recommendation with topic/identity review and combined reproducibility with human-interface claims. Seven questions now give each construct a primary outcome and an explicit evidence boundary.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=16mm,style=multiline]
  \item[RQ1] With what coverage, structural quality, and observable failure modes can the catalogue snapshot be transformed into an eligible page-aware corpus and complete authoritative indexes?
  \item[RQ2] How do keyword, feature-hashing, learned dense, heuristic hybrid, and development-tuned hybrid retrieval compare on held-out source-derived questions, and which heuristic terms materially change rankings?
  \item[RQ3] To what extent does the offline extractive answerer produce source-supported claims, correct claim--citation links, answer-point coverage, and appropriate abstention?
  \item[RQ4] Relative to an evidence-only baseline, what does AI-assisted proxy review reveal about the Thesis Extension Finder's relevance, source/suggestion separation, feasibility, scope, risk, and evaluation-plan quality?
  \item[RQ5] What quality and governance limits remain in controlled topic assignment, publication-derived author links, identity handling, and persisted generated-output review?
  \item[RQ6] To what extent can the bounded artefact and sanitized evidence package be reproduced and operationally characterized on documented local hardware, and what external format compatibility is demonstrated?
  \item[RQ7] To what extent do the implemented public and protected interfaces preserve evidence, uncertainty, privacy, authentication, correction, failure recovery, and automated accessibility contracts?
\end{description}

\section{Research objectives}

The questions correspond to seven objectives:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item establish one authoritative corpus/index boundary that fails loudly on missing, mismatched, partial, or stale state;
  \item compare retrieval families under one controlled split, with tuning and post-selection analysis clearly separated;
  \item evaluate answer support, attribution, completeness, and selective response as distinct constructs;
  \item implement and evaluate a thesis-extension workflow that keeps paper facts and system advice separate;
  \item provide inspectable topic/author discovery and attributable generated-output correction without unsafe identity or approval claims;
  \item bind code, corpus, model, experiment, document, and release evidence while characterizing local operational cost and external-format limits; and
  \item trace user-facing requirements through APIs, interface states, protected mutations, and automated tests without treating software verification as human usability evidence.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Claim boundaries}

RQ1 concerns the eligible subset and cannot make unavailable records searchable. RQ2 compares retrieval on source-derived AI-silver evidence and does not establish broader-corpus effectiveness or equivalence. RQ3 evaluates one deterministic extractive configuration; its intervals exclude AI-label uncertainty and it cannot represent every RAG generator. RQ4 evaluates synthetic profiles and an AI proxy; novelty, data access, time, prerequisite mastery, and supervisor suitability remain external. RQ5 measures a controlled vocabulary, source-backed relations, and review mechanics; it does not establish current author identity, availability, endorsement, or human approval.

RQ6 reports concurrency-one local measurements, restricted-content-aware reproduction, and a three-article external XML check. It does not establish service capacity, PDF transfer, or broad generalization. RQ7 verifies coded contracts and automated regressions. It does not establish comprehension, trust, satisfaction, accessibility conformance, or usability with assistive technology. The complete method--evidence--result--conclusion mapping appears in Appendix~\ref{app:traceability}.
