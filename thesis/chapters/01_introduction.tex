\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

Laboratory publication pages answer a bibliographic question---what has been published---but place the remaining analytical burden on the reader. A student must locate accessible full text, compare methods and limitations, identify a feasible extension, and avoid mistaking a generated proposal for an author-stated research gap. Researchers and public or industry users face related problems when locating work by topic or identifying a possible collaborator. The TTLAB precursor automated publication collection and display, lay summarisation, notifications, and a manual podcast workflow, and identified RAG querying as future work \cite{narine2025automating}. This project extends that direction from a publication archive into a full-text, source-traceable research-intelligence platform.

The wider scholarly-discovery landscape includes autonomous citation indexing in CiteSeer \cite{giles1998citeseer}, expert and topic mining in ArnetMiner \cite{tang2008arnetminer}, literature graphs and large corpora such as Semantic Scholar and S2ORC \cite{ammar2018literaturegraph,lo2020s2orc}, and open bibliographic infrastructure such as OpenAlex \cite{priem2022openalex}. These services demonstrate the value of linked scholarly metadata at scale. The present project addresses a narrower setting: a local laboratory corpus, full-text evidence with page and chunk locators, student-facing extension structures, public explanation, and explicit review state.

RAG can condition generation on retrieved documents \cite{lewis2020rag}, but a citation-bearing interface is not itself proof of retrieval completeness, entailment, recommendation feasibility, or user benefit. This distinction motivates the thesis's evidence boundary. Software tests establish implemented behavior. Corpus manifests establish coverage. Source-reviewed cases establish bounded empirical quality. Human usefulness, author endorsement, supervisor fit, and institutional approval require evidence that was not collected in this study.

\section{Aim}

The aim is to design, implement, and reproducibly evaluate a source-traceable research-intelligence platform that converts a bounded laboratory publication snapshot into searchable full text, citation-bearing answers, evidence-separated thesis-extension suggestions, and reviewable topic/author relations, while making failures and evidential limits visible.

\section{Objectives}

The objectives were to:

\begin{enumerate}[label=O\arabic*.,leftmargin=13mm]
  \item construct a deterministic, page-aware pipeline from catalog records and permitted local PDFs to eligible chunks and authoritative indexes;
  \item implement keyword, lexical feature-hashing, learned dense, and hybrid retrieval over one frozen corpus;
  \item preserve source identifiers and review state through search, RAG, recommendation, generated-artifact, and user-interface workflows;
  \item evaluate retrieval, claim support, citations, answer completeness, recommendation structure, controlled topics, author identity, generated outputs, and performance using persisted raw evidence;
  \item implement review, privacy, authentication, security, accessibility, and data-freshness controls appropriate to a demonstrable public-facing prototype; and
  \item provide one-command reproduction and a versioned sanitized release that excludes restricted full text and secrets.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Contributions}

The thesis makes five bounded contributions:

\begin{enumerate}[label=C\arabic*.,leftmargin=13mm]
  \item a layered research-intelligence artefact joining deterministic acquisition, page-aware chunking, four retrieval modes, answer and recommendation workflows, controlled topic/author discovery, and attributable review;
  \item an authoritative index-manifest design that binds corpus identity, ordered chunk/source hashes, model configuration, code commit, index hash, and completeness state, with atomic writes and fail-loud validation;
  \item reproducible source-derived evaluation sets and raw outputs for retrieval, QA faithfulness/citations, recommendations, topics/authors, generated-output review, section detection, performance, and an external format sanity check;
  \item empirical evidence that simple keyword retrieval had the highest held-out point estimates in this corpus, without an equivalence claim for dense retrieval, and that high extractive claim support did not imply adequate citation selection, answer completeness, or abstention; and
  \item an evidence-calibrated responsible-AI interface and sanitized release process that separates source facts, paper-stated future work, inference, suggestions, AI review, and external human validation.
\end{enumerate}

No new retrieval algorithm, human-validated recommendation benefit, researcher endorsement, production-scale capacity, or cross-institutional generalisation is claimed.
