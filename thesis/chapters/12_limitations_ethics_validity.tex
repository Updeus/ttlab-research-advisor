\chapter{Limitations, Ethics and Threats to Validity}
\label{ch:limitations}

\section{Technical and empirical limitations}

Only \EligiblePaperCount{} of \CatalogPaperCount{} catalog records enter full-text experiments. Thirty-six lack eligible text and two suspected PDF/title mismatches require external resolution. Native extraction is heuristic; no frozen-corpus document used OCR. The 199 unknown-section chunks constrain section-aware ranking. Metadata lacks verified DOI, abstract, and keyword coverage for many records. Thirteen author identity candidates remain unresolved.

The retrieval set has 50 AI-silver cases and only 20 held-out cases. Its single held-out unanswerable case makes the false-positive rate unstable. AI-created relevance pools may omit relevant papers; unjudged documents are treated as non-relevant. The strongest cumulative ablation was inspected after final configuration selection. Confidence intervals omit label and corpus-selection uncertainty.

QA results are derived from one offline extractive answerer and one AI-review process. High support partly reflects extraction rather than synthesis ability. Citation correctness and answer coverage are weak, and all unanswerable cases received answers. The local Ollama service was unavailable, so no generative-model comparison exists. Recommendation and topic findings are AI-proxy/silver results, not student, supervisor, researcher, or public judgments. The performance benchmark uses concurrency one, three cold and three warm repetitions, one machine, and a local WSL2 environment; it is not capacity or production testing.

\section{Ethics, permissions, and responsible AI}

TTLAB explicitly authorized the project and use of the laboratory corpus. This fact does not establish research-ethics-board approval or exemption, ownership of third-party publications, or blanket redistribution permission. No human participants were recruited; no satisfaction, usability, educational benefit, or supervisor-approval claim is made. If a later human study is undertaken, institutional ethics determination, informed consent, recruitment, retention, compensation, and withdrawal procedures must precede data collection.

Potential harms include source misrepresentation, inappropriate thesis advice, expertise misattribution, copyright overexposure, privacy leakage from project profiles, malicious documents, and unauthorised review changes. Controls include source locators, fact/inference/suggestion labels, AI/human review separation, warnings, evidence-only mode, transient profiles, authenticated mutations, append-only event hashes, PDF/downloader limits, and a sanitized release. These controls reduce risk; they do not amount to institutional approval or human verification.

Codex/LLM assistance was material in coding, evidence inspection, test and evaluation preparation, AI-silver labeling, analysis, and manuscript editing. Reviewer IDs, prompts or prompt hashes, seeds, and limitations are persisted. The exact underlying Codex model digest was not exposed by the runtime and was not invented. This disclosure should be reconciled with the University's current submission policy by the author/institution before formal submission.

\section{Construct validity}

Set Recall@$k$, Hit@$k$, MRR, nDCG, citation correctness, citation completeness, supported-claim rate, answer-point coverage, and abstention are defined separately. ``Grounded'' in operational records denotes structural checks, not entailment. Topic score denotes controlled or prototype label evidence, not topic truth. Recommendation score denotes heuristic fit, not usefulness, novelty, or feasibility. AI-review consistency is not human inter-rater reliability. Index coverage and test counts are engineering properties, not effectiveness.

\section{Internal validity}

Development/test separation reduces direct tuning leakage, and the retrieval runner freezes candidate pools and controls. Nevertheless, source-derived questions and labels were produced within one remediation process and may reflect the system's corpus vocabulary. The same AI reviewer created and verified labels, even though order and review lens changed. Error taxonomy tags are diagnostic signals and do not establish causal mechanisms. Background load, WSL scheduling, caches, and remote-server behavior influence performance.

\section{External validity}

The system and controlled taxonomy are tailored to one laboratory, English-language records, and a small local corpus. Archive markup, disciplinary terminology, PDF quality, and user goals will differ elsewhere. The three-document Europe PMC check covers only CC BY life-science JATS/XML and trivial lexical queries. It cannot support claims about other institutions, languages, domains, or large corpora. No human study supports generalisation to student decision-making or public understanding.

\section{Reproducibility validity}

Code, schemas, labels, raw sanitized results, model revision/hash, environment metadata, seeds, and manifests are versioned. Index validators bind derived state to the corpus. Exact replication still depends on lawful source access, changing remote archives, availability of the pinned model artifact, operating-system/toolchain differences, and externally excluded full-text review prompts. The release distinguishes open analytical reproduction from conditional full-corpus reproduction rather than claiming that both are identical.

\section{Security and deployment limitations}

The repository provides a fail-closed production configuration boundary, but it is not a hosted service. TLS, reverse proxy, identity-provider lifecycle, central monitoring, backups, incident response staffing, legal notices, accessibility conformance assessment, and operational correction processes remain deployment-specific. External IEEE PDF Express/Checker validation and any venue/institutional declarations require credentials and authorized human attestations.
