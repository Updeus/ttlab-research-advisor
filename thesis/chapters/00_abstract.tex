\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Publication archives expose bibliographic records but do not directly support full-text discovery, evidence-linked question answering, author/topic exploration, or the formulation of bounded thesis extensions. This thesis presents the design and controlled offline evaluation of a source-traceable research-intelligence platform for a TTLAB laboratory corpus. Source-traceable means that paper, chunk, page, section, provider, timestamp, warning, and review-state identifiers persist through retrieval and generated-output workflows; it does not mean that every claim is factually entailed.

The executed methodology combines an artefact-oriented engineering case study with source-derived, two-pass AI-reviewed silver evaluation. A frozen snapshot contains \CatalogPaperCount{} catalog records, \AvailablePdfCount{} available PDFs, \EligiblePaperCount{} eligible full-text papers, and \EligibleChunkCount{} eligible chunks after excluding \MismatchPaperCount{} PDF/title mismatches. Keyword, 256-dimensional feature-hashing, and a pinned 384-dimensional \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} dense index each cover \EligibleChunkCount{}/\EligibleChunkCount{} eligible chunks. On a held-out set of \RetrievalTestCount{} questions, keyword retrieval achieved set Recall@3 of \RetrievalKeywordRecallThree{}, MRR \RetrievalKeywordMrr{}, and nDCG@10 \RetrievalKeywordNdcgTen{}; the tuned hybrid achieved \RetrievalTunedHybridRecallThree{}, \RetrievalTunedHybridMrr{}, and \RetrievalTunedHybridNdcgTen{}, respectively. Corrected paired tests did not demonstrate hybrid superiority. All modes failed to abstain on the single held-out unanswerable case.

AI-assisted review of \QaClaimCount{} checkable claims from \QaCaseCount{} offline extractive answers found a strict supported-claim rate of \QaSupportedClaimRate{} (95\% CI \QaSupportedClaimRateLow{}--\QaSupportedClaimRateHigh{}) and complete citation presence, but citation correctness was \QaCitationCorrectness{} and strict answer-point coverage only \QaAnswerPointCoverage{}. A \RecommendationProfileCount{}-profile AI-proxy study found a small, inconclusive relevance difference of \RecommendationRelevanceDifference{} (95\% CI \RecommendationDifferenceLow{}--\RecommendationDifferenceHigh{}) between the full extension finder and an evidence-only baseline. Topic evaluation favored the controlled lexical method's precision (\TopicLexicalPrecision{}) over a dense prototype (\TopicDensePrecision{}), while neither established human-validated expertise. These negative and mixed results show that visible provenance and high local claim support can coexist with incomplete answers, imperfect citation choice, and weak abstention. The principal contribution is therefore a reproducible, evidence-calibrated platform and evaluation package, not a claim of validated student benefit.
