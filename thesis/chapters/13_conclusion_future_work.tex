\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

This thesis examined how a laboratory publication archive can become a source-traceable research-intelligence workflow. The implemented prototype converts 98 of 134 records into 756 page-aware chunks, offers searchable and citation-returning interfaces, separates evidence from thesis-extension suggestions, links papers to topics and author strings, generates reviewable artefacts, and exposes evaluation state. Its repository can be tested, probed, built, and audited using the supplied evidence package.

The bounded answers to the research questions are as follows. RQ1: the pipeline provides internally consistent extraction for 73.13\% of the snapshot, with missing PDFs, absent OCR, and incomplete section recognition. RQ2: retrieval and Ask TTLAB preserve paper/chunk/page provenance, but the active hashing index covers only 3.31\% of chunks and structural grounding does not establish correctness. RQ3: recommendation contracts distinguish source gap status from system suggestions, but no relevance or feasibility study supports the scores. RQ4: topics, authors, outputs, and review queues are inspectable, while identity defects, absent authentication, and zero review events prevent governance claims. RQ5: the audited implementation is reproducible from documented artefacts, but empirical effectiveness remains unevaluated.

The principal conclusion is not that the platform is already an effective research advisor. It is that a demonstrable, evidence-aware prototype exists and its own data makes the next scientific work clear.

\section{Prioritized future work}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Freeze and repair evidence state.} Rebuild the full vector index, clear stale statuses transactionally, record corpus/index/model manifests, and make the API expose coverage by mode.
  \item \textbf{Improve corpus quality.} Resolve author identities, remove parsing artefacts, populate verified metadata, acquire permitted missing PDFs, add OCR with confidence diagnostics, and validate section extraction.
  \item \textbf{Execute a gold retrieval/QA evaluation.} Establish reviewer-approved cases, compare modes and ablations, label claim--citation entailment, and report uncertainty and errors.
  \item \textbf{Evaluate recommendations with stakeholders.} Separate novelty, feasibility, source fidelity, usefulness, risk calibration, and evaluation-plan quality; compare against a retrieval-only baseline.
  \item \textbf{Complete governance.} Add authenticated review, reviewer identity, versioned approvals, change provenance, correction requests, retention/deletion controls, and deployment security.
  \item \textbf{Evaluate learned alternatives.} Compare the current lexical hashing baseline with a reproducibly versioned scholarly or sentence encoder, rather than assuming semantic improvement \cite{reimers2019sentencebert,cohan2020specter,thakur2021beir}.
  \item \textbf{Improve public communication.} Repair deep links and dashboard inconsistencies, hide raw markers, expose extraction review, and conduct accessibility and usability testing.
\end{enumerate}
