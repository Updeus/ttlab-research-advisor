\chapter{Problem Statement}
\label{ch:problem}

The practical problem is a mismatch between a publication archive and the questions its users need answered. A student may ask which paper matches a set of interests and constraints, what the paper actually did, which extension is new system advice, and how the extension could be evaluated. A researcher may want an accurate technical or public summary. A public or industry user may seek publications related to a problem or authors whose indexed work provides evidence of a possible connection. These answers require evidence distributed across full papers, metadata, authorship relations, and generated interpretation.

Four risks make a naive archive chatbot unsuitable. First, metadata-only retrieval omits methods, results, limitations, and future work present only in the paper body. Second, PDF acquisition and extraction can silently introduce missing text, scanned pages, malformed metadata, or document-identity errors. Third, generation can merge retrieved facts with inferred gaps and novel suggestions. Fourth, a polished interface can conceal incomplete indexes, weak abstention, low answer coverage, unresolved identities, or unreviewed outputs. RAG supplies evidence to a generator \cite{lewis2020rag}; it does not eliminate these risks.

The research problem is therefore:

\begin{quote}
How can a small, locally operated system transform a laboratory publication snapshot into a reproducible and inspectable research-intelligence workflow in which full-text evidence, generated interpretation, review state, evaluation state, and residual uncertainty remain distinguishable?
\end{quote}

\section{Scope}

The implemented scope comprises a \CatalogPaperCount{}-record catalog; permitted local PDFs; deterministic and optional OCR-aware extraction; page-aware chunks; SQLite persistence; React/Vite presentation; FastAPI services; keyword, feature-hashing, learned dense, and hybrid retrieval; an offline extractive answerer and optional local Ollama adapter; evidence-only and full extension workflows; controlled topic/author discovery; generated summaries and text podcast scripts; AI and human review states; and offline evaluation.

The study does not include unrestricted crawling, paywall bypass, hosted proprietary generation, participant recruitment, supervisor validation, researcher endorsement, audio synthesis, capacity/load testing, or production deployment. The optional OCR path is implemented and tested, but the frozen corpus records no OCR-completed pages; no OCR performance result is reported. The platform name uses the laboratory identifier present in repository and archive evidence without asserting an unverified acronym expansion.

\section{Operational definitions}

\emph{Source-traceable} means that an output retains persistent paper, chunk, page/section, provider/model, timestamp, warning, and review-state fields. \emph{Structurally grounded} means required citation fields and retrieved-chunk membership pass deterministic checks. Neither term is synonymous with factual entailment. \emph{AI-reviewed silver} denotes labels created and verified in two passes by the same declared AI reviewer process; it is not human gold data or human inter-rater agreement. \emph{Eligible corpus} denotes records with a verified or accepted PDF/text identity under the frozen inclusion rules; catalog records without text and suspected PDF/title mismatches remain visible but are excluded from retrieval experiments.

\section{Success criteria}

Engineering success requires deterministic processing, complete authoritative indexes for every eligible chunk, stable provenance fields, explicit degraded states, protected review mutations, reproducible builds, and a sanitized release. Empirical success is evaluated separately: retrieval quality relative to baselines, atomic claim support, citation correctness and completeness, answer-point coverage, abstention, recommendation proxy criteria, controlled-topic performance, section-label quality, review consistency, and bounded performance. Human usefulness remains outside the evidential scope.
