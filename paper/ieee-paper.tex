\begin{abstract}
Publication lists expose outputs but do not directly support full-text discovery, evidence-linked question answering, or the formulation of bounded student projects. This paper reports an artefact-oriented engineering case study and controlled offline evaluation of a source-traceable research-intelligence platform for a laboratory corpus. The frozen catalogue contains \PaperCatalogCount{} records and \PaperPdfCount{} locally available PDFs; title/content validation and extraction rules admit \PaperEligiblePaperCount{} papers and \PaperEligibleChunkCount{} page-aware chunks to the experimental indexes. The platform combines keyword search, a reproducible feature-hashing baseline, a pinned learned-dense encoder, hybrid retrieval, citation-returning extractive question answering, evidence-only and generated thesis-extension modes, controlled topic and author discovery, and append-only AI-review events. On a \PaperRetrievalCaseCount{}-case AI-reviewed silver set with creation and shuffled reinspection, held-out keyword retrieval achieved Recall@10~\PaperKeywordRecallTen{} and MRR~\PaperKeywordMrr{}, while the development-tuned hybrid achieved \PaperTunedHybridRecallTen{} and \PaperTunedHybridMrr{}; no Holm-corrected comparison established hybrid superiority. Claim review over \PaperQaCaseCount{} answers found a supported-claim rate of \PaperQaSupport{} but citation correctness of \PaperQaCitationCorrectness{} and answer-point coverage of only \PaperQaAnswerCoverage{}. A \PaperRecommendationProfileCount{}-profile AI-proxy recommendation comparison produced an uncertain relevance difference of \PaperRecDelta{} (95\% CI \PaperRecDeltaCi). Eligible index coverage was \PaperEligibleChunkCount{}/\PaperEligibleChunkCount{}, and citations were attached to \PaperQaCitationAttachedCount{}/\PaperQaClaimCount{} checkable claims; these traceability properties do not repair weak completeness or abstention and do not establish human advisory benefit. The contribution is a reproducible platform and evaluation protocol that separates traceability, empirical quality, and human validation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Institutional publication archives make research visible, but a title-and-link list leaves several practical questions unanswered: which full-text passages address a topic, which evidence supports an answer, how a prior study might be extended, and what remains unverified. Scholarly search engines and graphs support document and expert discovery at large scale \cite{giles1998citeseer,tang2008arnetminer,ammar2018literaturegraph,priem2022openalex}; research-paper recommenders add content or profile matching \cite{beel2016researchpaper,sugiyama2010scholarly}; and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) conditions generation on retrieved evidence \cite{lewis2020rag}. Combining these capabilities in a small, reviewable laboratory system creates a different engineering problem: the system must preserve provenance and uncertainty even when metadata, PDFs, models, and human review are incomplete.

The TTLAB precursor collected and displayed publications, generated lay summaries and notifications, and supported a manual podcast workflow; it described RAG as future work rather than an evaluated component \cite{narine2025automating}. The present system extends that workflow to full-text ingestion, page-aware retrieval, question answering, thesis-extension suggestions, topic/author exploration, and administrative review. We define \emph{source-traceable} narrowly: a returned claim or suggestion retains persistent paper, chunk, page, section, provider, timestamp, warning, and review-state locators. Traceability enables checking; it does not imply entailment, factual correctness, novelty, feasibility, or endorsement.

The study asks:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RQ1:} Can a heterogeneous laboratory archive be converted into an internally consistent, provenance-preserving experimental corpus and complete indexes?
  \item \textbf{RQ2:} How do keyword, feature-hashing, learned-dense, heuristic-hybrid, and development-tuned hybrid retrieval compare on source-derived silver judgments?
  \item \textbf{RQ3:} To what extent are extractive answers supported, correctly cited, complete, and able to abstain?
  \item \textbf{RQ4:} Does the full Thesis Extension Finder improve an evidence-only baseline under an AI-assisted proxy review, what is held-out topic-label performance, and what author-identity/integrity limits remain?
  \item \textbf{RQ5:} To what extent is the bounded artefact reproducible and reviewable on documented local hardware, externally format-compatible, and governed by privacy-minimizing, authenticated, accessible evidence interfaces without redistributing restricted PDFs?
\end{itemize}

The contributions are: (1) an authoritative corpus/index manifest that fails loudly on partial or stale derived state; (2) a modular platform that keeps source facts, paper-stated future work, inferred gaps, and system suggestions distinct; (3) executed retrieval, claim/citation, recommendation, topic, section, performance, and generated-output evaluations with raw evidence; and (4) a sanitized, hash-verified reproduction and release workflow. No recruited human participants were used, so usefulness and supervisor suitability remain outside the evidence.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Scholarly Discovery and Recommendation}
Early digital-library systems such as CiteSeer automated scholarly-document acquisition and indexing \cite{giles1998citeseer}. ArnetMiner and later scholarly graphs linked documents, authors, and topics for expert and literature discovery \cite{tang2008arnetminer,ammar2018literaturegraph}; OpenAlex exposes a broad open catalogue of scholarly entities and relationships \cite{priem2022openalex}. S2ORC shows the value and difficulty of large structured full-text corpora \cite{lo2020s2orc}. These systems motivate discovery and identity functions, but a laboratory-scale deployment also needs explicit review state, source permissions, and failure diagnostics.

Research-paper recommendation commonly combines content, citation, and user signals, while evaluation is sensitive to incomplete relevance judgments and unrealistic protocols \cite{beel2016researchpaper,sugiyama2010scholarly}. Expert finding similarly infers candidate expertise from document evidence rather than availability or endorsement \cite{balog2006formal,balog2009language}. Accordingly, this platform labels author-topic links as publication-derived evidence and does not present them as assignment decisions.

Table~\ref{tab:related-comparison} positions the implemented platform against representative scholarly-discovery systems using only capabilities supported by their cited descriptions. The comparison is intentionally functional rather than a claim of quality superiority.

\subsection{Retrieval, RAG, and Evaluation}
BM25-style lexical ranking remains a strong information-retrieval baseline \cite{robertson2009bm25}. Feature hashing provides a memory-bounded sparse representation but is not a learned semantic encoder \cite{weinberger2009featurehashing}. Sentence-BERT, DPR, and SPECTER demonstrate learned representations for sentence, passage, and scholarly-document retrieval \cite{reimers2019sentencebert,karpukhin2020dpr,cohan2020specter}; BEIR shows that relative performance varies materially across tasks and domains \cite{thakur2021beir}. This motivates comparison rather than assuming dense or hybrid superiority.

RAG retrieves evidence before generation \cite{lewis2020rag}; variants investigate retrieval/generation coupling and self-reflection \cite{izacard2021fid,asai2024selfrag}. Returned citations, however, do not alone establish claim support. RAGAS and ARES operationalize automated RAG evaluation, and FActScore decomposes generated text into atomic factual units \cite{es2024ragas,saadfalcon2024ares,min2023factscore}. We adapt atomic-claim inspection but identify all judgments as AI-assisted silver evidence rather than human ground truth.

\subsection{Topic Models and Human Review}
Probabilistic LDA and embedding-assisted BERTopic offer corpus-induced topics, but topic coherence does not by itself guarantee semantic interpretability \cite{blei2003lda,grootendorst2022bertopic,doogan2021topic}. Interactive machine-learning guidance further argues that people must be able to inspect, correct, and understand model behavior throughout the workflow \cite{amershi2014power}. This project therefore retains an inspectable controlled vocabulary, compares it with a pinned dense prototype, and records attributed review transitions rather than presenting automatically assigned labels as final truth.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Design and Units of Analysis}
The work is an artefact-oriented engineering case study with design-science characteristics plus controlled offline evaluation. The artefact is the repository, database schema, indexes, API, React interface, review log, experiment code, and documentation. Units of analysis are papers and chunks for corpus integrity; queries for retrieval; atomic claims, citation links, and answer points for QA; synthetic student profiles and ranked paper sets for recommendation; papers for topic classification; and pipeline stages for performance. The study reports software/document experiments and AI-assisted review only. TTLAB authorization to undertake the project and use the laboratory corpus is recorded as a project fact; it is not presented as research-ethics approval or blanket third-party redistribution permission.

\subsection{Corpus Freeze and Inclusion}
The frozen snapshot \texttt{\PaperCorpusSnapshot} begins with \PaperCatalogCount{} catalogue records and \PaperPdfCount{} locally available PDFs. A paper enters the experimental corpus only when its metadata record, permitted local PDF, extracted text, and title/content checks are consistent. \PaperNoTextCount{} records remain unavailable or need review, and \PaperMismatchCount{} PDFs are excluded because their contents do not match the catalogue titles. The resulting \PaperEligiblePaperCount{} eligible papers produce \PaperEligibleChunkCount{} eligible chunks from \PaperRawChunkCount{} raw chunks. Each chunk stores page range, inferred section, text hash, and source hash. Unknown sections are retained rather than forced.

Keyword FTS and vector manifests are generated over the same ordered eligible-chunk list. Every manifest records the corpus snapshot/hash, configuration hash, code commit, model/revision, dimensions, normalization, index hash, ordered chunk hashes, and completeness. Atomic replacement precedes transactional status updates; a bounded demonstration index is isolated and cannot claim authoritative coverage. Startup, API diagnostics, and experiments reject count, hash, model, or status mismatches.

\subsection{Silver Evaluation Procedure}
The retrieval set contains \PaperRetrievalCaseCount{} source-derived questions stratified across factual lookup, methods, results, limitations/future work, bibliographic lookup, topics/applications, comparison, broad intent, multi-paper synthesis, hard negatives, and unanswerable cases. One AI process created evidence-linked judgments; a later shuffled pass re-inspected source pages without system rankings. The \PaperRetrievalDevCount{}/\PaperRetrievalTestCount{} development/test split was fixed before tuning. This is annotation-consistency checking, not inter-rater agreement or human gold labeling.

For query $q$, judged relevant papers are $G_q$ and the first $k$ predicted papers are $P_{q,k}$. Set recall is
\begin{equation}
R_q@k=\frac{|G_q\cap P_{q,k}|}{|G_q|},
\end{equation}
while Hit@$k$ is the indicator that the intersection is non-empty. We also report Precision@$k$, reciprocal rank, and nDCG@10 for graded judgments. Aggregates use answerable queries; abstention and false-positive rates use unanswerable queries. \PaperRetrievalBootstrapRepetitions{} query-bootstrap resamples provide percentile 95\% intervals. Two-sided paired randomization tests compare the tuned hybrid against each baseline, with Holm-Bonferroni family correction.

The QA set reuses the same \PaperQaCaseCount{} questions. It evaluates the offline extractive sentence-overlap answerer with the current untuned heuristic-hybrid retriever; it must not be read as an evaluation of the best keyword or development-tuned retriever. Answers were segmented into atomic checkable claims, connected to returned citations and AI-reviewed silver reference answer points, then reviewed for support, citation correctness/completeness, answer-point coverage, unsupported general knowledge, and answerability. Recommendation evaluation uses \PaperRecommendationProfileCount{} synthetic profiles covering skills, interests, time, data, difficulty, project type, and avoid-topics. The evidence-only arm returns ranked papers/passages; the full arm additionally constructs an explicitly labeled extension. Topic evaluation uses a \PaperTopicCaseCount{}-case multi-label silver set, including all \PaperTopicControlledLabelCount{} controlled labels and other/unknown cases, with \PaperTopicDevCount{} development and \PaperTopicTestCount{} held-out cases. Generated-output review inspects every \PaperReviewAnswerCount{} historical answers, \PaperReviewRecommendationCount{} recommendation runs, and \PaperReviewArtifactCount{} paper artefacts, appending attributed hash-chained events.

\section{System Design and Implementation}
Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} separates presentation, API/security, intelligence, and evidence state. The React/Vite frontend provides deep-linkable Dashboard, Papers, Search, Ask TTLAB, Thesis Extension Finder, Topic/Author Explorer, Evaluation, and Admin routes. It exposes index freshness, source snippets, generation provider/time, warnings, review state/type, and evidence-only alternatives. Profile inputs remain in browser/request memory unless a user explicitly invokes a persistence-capable workflow; the evaluated finder calls are non-persistent.

FastAPI exposes public read-only discovery and bounded generation endpoints. Reviewer/admin changes require an environment-configured actor and authenticated token. There is no committed default secret. The security boundary applies exact origin/host configuration, request and upload limits, rate controls, path and URL validation, PDF-bomb safeguards, redacted logging, protected review transitions, and append-only attributed audit events. Local-demo mode is explicit and is not described as production authentication.

PyMuPDF extraction records per-page diagnostics. Blank/scanned/mixed detection and an optional OCR adapter preserve page method and review state; the current frozen corpus used no OCR, so OCR quality is supported only by engineering fixtures. The section detector uses heading/layout evidence and preserves \texttt{Unknown}; a \PaperSectionCaseCount{}-case AI-reviewed silver sample measured \PaperSectionAccuracy{} exact accuracy, while \PaperUnknownChunkCount{}/\PaperEligibleChunkCount{} eligible chunks remain unknown.

Retrieval modes share eligibility and filter controls. Keyword ranking uses SQLite FTS. The \PaperHashingDimension{}-dimensional signed feature-hashing path is retained as a lexical-feature baseline. Learned dense retrieval uses the \PaperDenseDimension{}-dimensional all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence transformer at the revision pinned in the released index manifest, with L2 normalization and mean-pooled overlapping windows for long chunks. Model acquisition is explicit; an unavailable dense model produces a visible fallback rather than silent substitution. The heuristic hybrid uses pre-existing keyword/vector weights and \PaperHeuristicTermCount{} hand-authored expansion/boost/penalty terms. A separate development search selected keyword/dense weights \PaperTunedKeywordWeight{}/\PaperTunedDenseWeight{} without consulting the held-out test set.

\section{Discussion}
The principal engineering result is not a new retrieval algorithm but an evidence boundary that remains intact across ingestion, ranking, generation, review, API serialization, and user interface. RQ1 is answered positively for the frozen eligible subset: \PaperEligibleChunkCount{} chunks have one-to-one coverage in all authoritative indexes, and stale/partial indexes fail validation. This does not make the \PaperNoTextCount{} unavailable or review-required records searchable or correct missing metadata.

RQ2 yields a useful negative result. Learned-dense MRR was \PaperDenseMrr{} versus keyword MRR \PaperKeywordMrr{}; equivalence was not tested. Both heuristic and tuned hybrids had lower point estimates than the keyword baseline on this silver set, and the corrected paired analysis found no significant advantage. The result cautions against using the word ``hybrid'' as a quality claim and against treating feature hashing as semantic retrieval. The ablation pattern suggests that hand-authored topical adjustments can damage ranking even when they appear intuitively helpful.

RQ3 distinguishes faithfulness from usefulness. Extractive answers rarely invented unsupported material, yet often selected the wrong supported sentences, attached only partially correct citations, omitted answer points, and failed to abstain. Structural source traceability therefore makes failure inspectable but does not resolve it. A future answerer should use explicit answerability thresholds, retrieval calibrated to question intent, within-paper evidence selection, and claim-to-source verification before presentation.

RQ4 shows that recommendation structure can be auditable without being validated advice. All \PaperRecommendationItemsPerArm{} AI-proxy-reviewed full-finder outputs kept evidence and suggestion fields separate, but the relevance difference is uncertain and feasibility remains unconfirmed. Likewise, controlled topics and publication-derived author links are implemented navigation features that may aid discovery; they are not proof of complete expertise. Human student, supervisor, author, and accessibility studies are deliberately left as future ethically governed work.

RQ5 is positive only within the authorized local boundary. The 17-stage, three-cold/three-warm, concurrency-one profile completed all 102 samples without failure on the documented WSL2 host; it is not load or capacity evidence. The clean-commit full reproduction and sanitized release verifiers passed while excluding PDFs, the live database, derived text, interface screenshots, secrets, and model files. The external harness mapped three CC BY JATS/XML articles into the production chunker contract and achieved 3/3 fixed lexical top-one matches; it did not test PDF ingestion or cross-domain quality. Public questions/profiles are transient and mutations authenticated. Automated accessibility regressions passed, but WCAG conformance and assistive-technology usability were not established.

\section{Threats, Ethics, and Responsible Deployment}
\textbf{Construct validity:} silver relevance, AI claim judgments, and proxy recommendation ratings may not measure human usefulness, trust, novelty, or feasibility. Citation correctness is claim-to-evidence support, not truth beyond the source. Performance and test counts are engineering properties, not effectiveness metrics.

\textbf{Internal validity:} within each two-pass silver dataset, the same AI procedure created and re-inspected labels without independent human adjudication, so correlated errors can survive both passes. Small held-out subsets create wide or degenerate intervals, especially the single retrieval unanswerable. Post-selection ablations are descriptive. The offline extractive answerer avoids stochastic model variance but is not representative of all RAG generators.

\textbf{External validity:} results concern one laboratory snapshot and a small external format sanity check, not scholarly search generally. Thirty-six records have no eligible text and two mismatched PDFs were excluded. Topic vocabulary, author identities, English-language extraction, and local hardware further limit transfer.

\textbf{Reproducibility validity:} pinned inputs, revisions, seeds, raw rankings, manifests, and hashes reduce drift, but network discovery and operating-system caches remain variable. The learned model must be acquired explicitly, and venue PDF validation requires external credentials.

TTLAB explicitly authorized the project and use of the laboratory corpus. No participants were recruited, and no ethics approval or exemption is claimed. Authorization does not confer rights to redistribute every third-party PDF; source PDFs, the live database, credentials, and private backups are excluded from the sanitized bundle. Student profile inputs are minimized and non-persistent by default. Public deployment requires authenticated review, controlled origins/hosts, rate limiting, corrections, backups, monitoring, and accessible interaction. Datasheet, model-card, and NIST AI risk-management practices inform the disclosure and governance approach \cite{gebru2021datasheets,mitchell2019modelcards,tabassi2023airmf}. Codex/LLM assistance was used for code, silver-label preparation, analysis, and manuscript editing and is explicitly disclosed; it was not a human participant or independent assessor.

\section{Availability and Conclusion}
The sanitized release contains code, permitted metadata, sanitized experimental records, redistributable configurations, code-native figure sources, manifests, checksums, and reproduction instructions. It excludes PDFs, the live SQLite database, derived-text caches, interface screenshots, private full-text prompts, secrets, model caches, and backups; no restricted-content redistribution is claimed.

For the frozen corpus, the platform makes research-intelligence failures inspectable and separates empirical quality from interface completeness. Complete eligible indexing, dense MRR \PaperDenseMrr{}, and claim support \PaperQaSupport{} coexist with a negative hybrid result, answer-point coverage \PaperQaAnswerCoverage{}, citation correctness \PaperQaCitationCorrectness{}, failed abstention, and an uncertain recommendation difference. It is therefore an evaluated research artefact and recommendation prototype, not a validated academic advisor. Human usefulness, identity decisions, third-party rights, and venue compliance remain external validations rather than values to invent.
