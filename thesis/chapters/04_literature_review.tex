\chapter{Literature Review}
\label{ch:literature}

\section{Scholarly discovery and research intelligence}

CiteSeer demonstrated autonomous acquisition, citation extraction, and search over online papers \cite{giles1998citeseer}. ArnetMiner combined extraction and mining for expert and topic discovery \cite{tang2008arnetminer}. Semantic Scholar's literature graph, S2ORC, and OpenAlex illustrate complementary graph, full-text-corpus, and open-metadata infrastructures \cite{ammar2018literaturegraph,lo2020s2orc,priem2022openalex}. The TTLAB precursor automated publication collection, display, lay summaries, notifications, and manual podcast preparation, with RAG presented as future work \cite{narine2025automating}. The present artefact differs by retaining local full-text/page provenance, recommendation claim boundaries, review state, and offline quality evidence for one laboratory corpus.

Large scholarly systems offer scale and citation networks. A bounded laboratory system instead makes source passages and processing failures directly inspectable, but inherits corpus incompleteness and cannot claim broad coverage. This trade-off motivates an artefact evaluation focused on manifest integrity, evidence retention, and explicit limits.

\section{Scholarly recommendation and expert finding}

Research-paper recommendation combines content, citations, profiles, and hybrid signals; surveys identify reproducible evaluation and realistic user modelling as continuing challenges \cite{beel2016researchpaper}. Publication history can represent recent research interests \cite{sugiyama2010scholarly}. Expert finding has been formalised through candidate- and document-centric language models \cite{balog2006formal,balog2009language}. The platform is intentionally more conservative: it ranks papers for a declared project profile and derives possible author/topic connections only from indexed authorship. It does not infer willingness, availability, endorsement, or complete expertise.

\section{Lexical, hashed, and learned dense retrieval}

BM25 remains a strong lexical baseline \cite{robertson2009bm25}. Feature hashing projects sparse lexical features into a fixed memory footprint \cite{weinberger2009featurehashing}; it should not be described as a learned semantic encoder. Sentence-BERT learns sentence representations suitable for cosine comparison \cite{reimers2019sentencebert}; Dense Passage Retrieval learns query and passage representations for open-domain QA \cite{karpukhin2020dpr}; SPECTER uses citation signals for scholarly document representations \cite{cohan2020specter}. BEIR shows that zero-shot effectiveness varies by dataset and that dense retrieval should not be presumed superior \cite{thakur2021beir}. This thesis therefore compares keyword, 256-dimensional signed feature hashing, a pinned 384-dimensional Sentence-Transformer, and two hybrid configurations on identical cases.

\section{Topics and author--topic relations}

Latent Dirichlet allocation represents documents as distributions over latent topics \cite{blei2003lda}. BERTopic combines transformer embeddings, clustering, and class-based term weighting \cite{grootendorst2022bertopic}. Topic interpretability remains difficult to reduce to one numerical measure \cite{doogan2021topic}. For a small curated corpus, the project retains an editable 39-label controlled vocabulary and evaluates it against a dense label-prototype alternative. The design trades topic discovery for determinism and governance; measured precision, recall, and coverage are reported rather than assumed.

\section{RAG, claim support, and citation evaluation}

RAG conditions generation on retrieved documents \cite{lewis2020rag}. Fusion-in-Decoder aggregates evidence from multiple passages \cite{izacard2021fid}, while Self-RAG integrates retrieval and self-reflection decisions \cite{asai2024selfrag}. These architectures motivate evidence-aware generation but do not make displayed citations automatically correct.

RAGAS and ARES propose automated RAG evaluation dimensions and synthetic evaluation workflows \cite{es2024ragas,saadfalcon2024ares}. FActScore decomposes long-form text into atomic factual claims \cite{min2023factscore}. SummEval and G-Eval illustrate both the need for multidimensional review and the limitations of automatic or model-based judges \cite{fabbri2021summeval,liu2023geval}. Following that literature, this study segments offline answers into atomic claims, judges support and claim--citation links, measures answer-point coverage and abstention, and identifies the reviewer as AI. The procedure is formative silver evaluation, not a substitute for independent human assessment.

\section{Human review, documentation, and responsible AI}

Interactive machine learning emphasizes the role of feedback in system improvement \cite{amershi2014power}. Datasheets and model cards formalise provenance, intended use, and limitation reporting \cite{gebru2021datasheets,mitchell2019modelcards}. Language-model risk taxonomies include misinformation and interaction harms \cite{weidinger2022taxonomy}; the NIST AI Risk Management Framework structures governance around mapping, measurement, management, and oversight \cite{tabassi2023airmf}. The platform operationalises related controls through visible provenance, generated-content notices, separate AI/human review states, authenticated mutations, append-only event hashes, privacy minimisation, and explicit correction states.

\section{Research gap and positioning}

Prior work supplies mature components for scholarly graphs, recommendation, expert finding, retrieval, topic modelling, RAG, and evaluation. The gap addressed here is their evidence-calibrated integration for a small laboratory: full-paper ingestion; paper/chunk/page/section traceability; source-fact, future-work, inference, and suggestion separation; evidence-only and generative recommendation modes; attributable review; and an evaluation dashboard that can report failure rather than imply success. The contribution is an engineered and evaluated integration, not a new foundational model or validated educational intervention.
